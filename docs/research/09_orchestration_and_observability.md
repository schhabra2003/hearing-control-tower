## Summary

SCOPE AND CAVEATS. 30 tool calls used. All pages were read through a page-reading tool that returns extracted text; quotes are as relayed by it and prices mostly come from pricing tables, so spot-check any figure before it goes into a business case. Search results returned only titles and links, so nothing here rests on a snippet. Several items were not reached (see not_found).

1. IS THE PROTOTYPE DESIGN IN LINE WITH PUBLISHED GUIDANCE? Yes, on the sourced evidence.
- Workflow, not agent: Anthropic defines a workflow as model calls on predefined code paths and advises the simplest solution, possibly no agentic system at all. OpenAI says workflows add no new nondeterminism and each call can be evaluated in isolation.
- Two calls per message: this matches Anthropic's 'voting' form of parallelization, recommended when several attempts are needed for higher confidence.
- Deterministic verifier and human queue: Google classes user confirmation and similar checks as 'deterministic defenses'; OpenAI says untrusted data should never directly drive behaviour and tool approvals should stay on.
- Reading untrusted email and texts: the three vendors converge on (a) structured outputs to constrain what flows downstream, (b) keeping untrusted text out of system/developer messages (Anthropic: only in tool_result blocks), (c) an optional lightweight pre-screen, (d) monitoring outputs for successful injection, (e) layering. A prototype that puts the message body in the system prompt, or lets extracted text trigger an action without the verifier, would be out of line.
- Evaluation: OpenAI's guide asks for eval-driven development, logging everything, continuous evaluation on every change, and calibrating automated scores against human labels. The human review queue is the natural source of those labels (my inference).

2. OPENTELEMETRY. The GenAI conventions are still at Development status (per a Sept 2026 secondary source) and moved to their own repository in June 2026, with no release listed in that repo when read. Use the names (span 'chat <model>', gen_ai.provider.name, gen_ai.request.model, gen_ai.usage.input_tokens / output_tokens, metrics gen_ai.client.token.usage and gen_ai.client.operation.duration) but pin the instrumentation version and expect renames. No standard cost attribute was found: compute cost from tokens. Content capture is opt-in, which matters because the messages hold personal data.

3. SMALLEST TOOLING FOR A THREE-PERSON FIRM (my recommendation, built on the prices above; volume assumed to be a few thousand messages a month, which is an assumption to confirm).
- Tracing and evals: one tool. Langfuse is the only one checked that is both MIT-licensed for free self-hosting with all core features and cheap when hosted ($29/month Core; the free Hobby tier caps at 2 users). Hosted alternatives with a free tier: Arize AX Free (25k spans, 15 days), LangSmith Developer (5k traces, per seat), Braintrust Starter (1 GB, 14 days), Helicone Hobby (10k requests, 1 seat). LangSmith, Braintrust, Arize AX and Helicone reserve self-hosting or on-prem for Enterprise; Phoenix is the open-source local option from Arize.
- Durable execution: at this volume every option's free tier suffices (Inngest 50k executions, Restate 100k actions, Temporal $150 credit then $50 per million actions). The smallest footprint is a library on the Postgres the firm already needs (DBOS Transact, free and open source) or a plain Postgres job table; Temporal's $500/month Business plan and self-run cluster are out of proportion. I found no primary source for the Postgres queue-plus-outbox pattern within budget, so that part is engineering judgement, not a sourced fact.
- Avoid adding an agent framework: Anthropic notes they can hide the prompts.

4. METRICS. I found no primary source that lists metrics for extraction pipelines. What the sources do support: token usage and operation duration as standard OTel metrics (cost and latency percentiles derive from them), and OpenAI's 'maintain agreement' between automated scores and human feedback. Inter-model agreement rate, human-review rate, schema-failure rate and drift are sensible (my judgement) but unsourced here.
