## Summary

SCOPE AND LIMITS. 30 tool calls used. Pages were read through a fetch tool that summarises with a small model, so quotes are as relayed by it. Ownwell's own pricing page was blocked; its fee comes from trade press and a competitor page. No official source gave an average tax-dollar saving per successful residential appeal, and no source gave a per-hearing pay rate. Everything labelled "derived" below is my arithmetic on the sourced inputs, not a sourced number.

FEES. Contingency rates span roughly 25% to 50% of first-year savings: Ownwell about 25% (news), 25% to 35% by state (competitor page, unverified); O'Connor 50%, per its own terms. Flat-fee challengers sit at $49 to $129. On later years, the only primary evidence is O'Connor's evergreen agreement: it re-protests every year and charges on each year's savings until cancelled on 30 days' notice. No firm was found charging a multi-year tail on a single year's win.

WIN RATES (official). Cook County Board of Review, tax year 2024: 50.07% of appellants got a reduction (represented 48.04%, pro se 55.44%). King County 2012 to 2017: about 40%. Harris County publishes volume (516,205 protests in 2024) and a 6.98% average value reduction, but not a win rate. A planning range of 40% to 50% is defensible; anything higher rests on vendor claims.

SAVINGS. The only dollar figure found is Ownwell's claim of $774 average annual savings per customer; the base (all customers or winners only) is undefined.

HEARINGS ARE THE MINORITY PATH. At Travis CAD in 2025 only about 10% of residential protests reached a formal ARB hearing; about 84% closed by joint motion, topline or informal agreement. Agents filed 86% of all protests. So attended hearings are a small, probably harder slice of the caseload, and the average win rate and saving may not apply to them.

DERIVED VALUE OF ONE WON CASE. $774 x 25% = about $194; x 35% = about $271; x 50% = $387.

DERIVED EXPECTED FEE PER CASE (40% to 50% win rate). About $77 to $97 at a 25% fee; about $155 to $194 at 50%. Absent hearing-specific data, this is the best available proxy for the fee value of one attended hearing.

DERIVED COST OF ONE MISSED HEARING. At minimum the expected fee forgone ($77 to $194), plus the homeowner's lost saving (up to about $774 on the vendor figure), plus the lost renewal under an evergreen agreement. Whether a no-show forfeits the protest outright was not sourced here and should be checked per state.

DERIVED COORDINATOR HOUR. BLS May 2025 median of $23.23 an hour, divided by the 70.0% wage share in private-industry compensation (June 2026), gives about $33 loaded. For SOC 43-6014 alone ($22.86) it is about $33; for a paralegal ($30.24) about $43. The 1.43 multiplier is an all-firm average and likely overstates a small firm's benefit load, so $27 to $33 is a reasonable band.

DERIVED COMPARISON. One missed hearing costs the firm roughly 2 to 6 loaded coordinator hours in expected fee alone. The single consultant pay point ($75 to $100 an hour, advisory contract) is about equal to the expected fee from one hearing at a 25% fee, so the model only works if a consultant clears several hearings per paid hour or is paid well below that rate.
