# Levers that need no software

Evidence on reminders, wording, standby and cancellation pay, and the rules for texting.

> How to read this file. It was gathered on 18 November 2026 by an AI research assistant with web
> access, one topic per assistant, limited to 30 lookups. Pages were read through a tool that
> returns extracts, so every quote is as that tool relayed it. **Check a quote against its link

## Summary

Scope and method: 29 tool calls, about 13 pages opened. Quotes were extracted by the page-reading tool and should be spot-checked before publication. Two facts are marked unverified because I saw only a search-result title.

What the evidence supports

1. Reminders work, and texts are as good as calls. A Cochrane review found text reminders raise attendance by about 14% relative to no reminder (RR 1.14) and match phone-call reminders (RR 0.99), at 55-65% lower cost per attendance in two trials. A New York City court study found a redesigned summons plus text reminders cut failures to appear by 13-21%. Both are from patients and defendants, not paid contractors, so the size of the effect on consultants is an inference.

2. Wording matters at zero cost. In two NHS randomised trials (about 20,000 patients), a text stating the specific cost of a missed appointment cut no-shows from 11.1% to 8.4% against a standard reminder, a 23% relative reduction. The winning message had three parts: "we are expecting you", the cost of not attending, and a number to cancel or rearrange.

3. Money levers have thin but usable benchmarks.
- Late-cancellation pay: California courts may pay contract interpreters up to the half-day or full-day rate when the court cancels with under 24 hours' notice. The half-day minimum ($175) is 50% of the full-day fee ($350).
- Incentive size: a Chicago substitute-teacher study estimates incentives of about 50% of daily wages would be needed to close fill-rate gaps, which suggests small bonuses move fill rates only modestly.
- Standby pay: the only sourced share I found is the federal cap of 25% of basic pay for standby duty. That is a ceiling for salaried employees, not a market rate for freelancers.

4. Legal basics (confirmed from FCC text): AI-generated voices count as "artificial" under the TCPA (FCC 24-17, released 8 February 2024); such calls need prior express consent, and prior express written consent if telemarketing. On texting, I have only a 2022 vendor guide saying 10DLC brand and campaign registration, consumer consent and STOP opt-out handling are required.

5. Hearing offices: Harris Central Appraisal District (Texas) takes evidence electronically through its owner portal (recommended at least 3 days before the hearing) and must deliver communications electronically to a designated representative on request. I found no office stating it accepts agent submissions by email.

Week-one implications (my inference, not sourced findings)
- Send a manual text reminder to every consultant and homeowner before each hearing, using the three-part wording above with the firm's own cost of a no-show.
- Use texts rather than staff phone calls for routine reminders; reserve calls for non-responders.
- Write a simple fee rule modelled on the interpreter policy: a half-fee minimum, and a cancellation fee when the firm cancels inside 24 hours.
- If a standby consultant is paid, 25% of the normal fee is a defensible starting ceiling to test, with weak evidence behind it.
- Collect written texting consent on the homeowner engagement form and the consultant agreement, honour STOP, and avoid AI-voice calls until counsel confirms consent wording.

Main gaps: no trial evidence on asking for an explicit confirmation reply, no traceable two-way SMS response-rate data, and no gig-platform reliability figures were obtained.

## Looked for and not found

- Effect of asking for an explicit confirmation reply (versus a one-way reminder) on no-show rates: no randomised trial or review was opened. A pediatric clinic RCT (https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC5227159/) appeared in search results but was not opened.
- Response rates and response times for two-way SMS in scheduling: only vendor marketing pages surfaced; no traceable primary figure found.
- Gig staffing platform figures on reliability scores, cancellation penalties or reliability-linked pay (fill rates, no-show rates): the one company page tried (Instawork blog) redirected and was not opened within budget.
- Typical standby or on-call pay as a share of the normal fee for freelance contractors (interpreters, per-diem nurses): no source found; only the federal 25% cap for salaried employees.
- Penalties for a contractor who fails to appear: the California interpreter policy does not address this, and no other source was found.
- Text-only effect size in the Science court study: the abstract gives only the combined 13-21% range. A J-PAL rural text-reminder study (https://www.povertyactionlab.org/node/7802260) was seen in results but not opened.
- TCPA consent-revocation rule content (how fast an opt-out must be honoured, what counts as a reasonable opt-out): the Federal Register page failed to load. A 26 October 2026 Federal Register item on the same subject was not opened and may change current rules.
- A2P 10DLC requirements from an FCC or carrier source, and current registration fees or penalties for unregistered traffic: only a 2022 vendor guide was opened. The CTIA Messaging Principles document was not located directly.
- Any hearing office or court stating that it accepts email submissions from agents: not found. Harris Central Appraisal District's pages describe a portal for owners; its agent-specific pages (iFile, agent services) were not opened.
- Size of the Chicago substitute-teacher bonus and its measured effect on fill rates: not in the NBER abstract; the $40 figure comes from an unopened news headline.
- The PLOS ONE paper behind the NHS cost-message trials (https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4569397) was blocked by a browser check; figures are from the UK government summary.
