# Open and closed models for the reader: prices and benchmarks

What the public record says about open-weight and closed models for reading short messages into a fixed schema, and what they cost.

## Summary

Recommendation: make a closed model the first reader, test an open-weight model (DeepSeek V4.1 Flash on a pinned US host) as the second reader, and do not self-host. The public evidence does not settle accuracy on negation, hedging and conditions, so the second-reader choice has to be confirmed on your own labelled messages.

Cost does not decide this. My arithmetic for one message (3,000 tokens in, 600 out) from the list prices above:

| Model | Type | Per message | 10,000 messages a month |
|---|---|---|---|
| GPT-6 Luna | closed | $0.0003 (if the row is standard pricing) | $3 |
| DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, DeepInfra | open | $0.0005 | $5 |
| Claude Haiku 5.5 | closed | $0.0006 | $6 |
| DeepSeek V4.1 Flash, Together or Fireworks | open | $0.0016 | $16 |
| Gemini 3.1 Flash-Lite | closed | $0.0017 | $17 |
| GPT-6.1 Terra | closed | $0.0030 | $30 |
| GLM-5.3, Together | open | $0.0068 | $68 |
| Claude Sonnet 5.5 or GPT-6.2 Sol | closed | $0.012 | $120 |

The cheap closed models cost about the same as the cheapest open-weight one. Even two mid-tier closed readers come to about $240 a month at that volume. Reasoning tokens bill as output and would raise all of these.

What the benchmarks say:
- **General capability:** closed models lead clearly (58 against 32 to 33, or 46 if Kimi K3.1 Pro counts as open). That index measures hard reasoning, not short-message extraction.
- **Faithfulness:** on Vectara (22 October 2026) DeepSeek V4.1 Flash hallucinates at 4.2%, better than the closed mid-tier models listed. The current cheap closed models are not on that board.
- **Function calling and JSON schema:** nothing current. The Berkeley leaderboard dates from April 2026 and JSONSchemaBench from 2025.
- **Prompt injection:** the one 2026 benchmark opened shows wide spread within both camps and no clear open versus closed gap. Cheap models of either kind should not be trusted to resist injected instructions; the defence has to be in the design.

Why closed first:
- Strict structured outputs for open models are per host, not per model. DeepSeek's own endpoint, Novita and Alibaba do not list them, and Fireworks switches off reasoning output when a schema is enforced.
- Open models on OpenRouter are spread over 35 to 47 hosts, some in China. Homeowner data needs the host pinned, training opt-out set and the ZDR flag on.
- Neither open model checked has a Groq or Cerebras endpoint.

Why an open second reader is worth testing: it comes from a different model family at negligible cost, which is the point of requiring two readers to agree. This is a judgment; I found no measurement of how correlated the errors of open and closed models are. If it fails your test set or the data terms are unacceptable, use a second closed model from a different vendor.

Self-hosting loses at any plausible volume (my arithmetic):
- DeepSeek V4.1 Flash has 552B parameters. Four B200s at $7.99 an hour is about $23,300 a month and may not even hold it.
- That breaks even only above roughly 14 million messages a month against Together's price, or 50 million against DeepInfra's.
- A small model on one H100 ($2,110 a month) breaks even against Haiku 5.5 at about 3.5 million messages a month.

Quotes were extracted by a page-reading tool; table rows are as it relayed them. Re-check the OpenAI Terra and Luna rows and the Anthropic rows by eye before they go into the business case.
