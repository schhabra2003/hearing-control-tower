## Summary

Method note: I used all 30 tool calls. Pages were read through a fetch tool that returns extracts, so quotes are as that tool reported them; table figures (Travis) are chart or table values, not sentences. Where I add arithmetic or inference it is labelled.

HOW SEASONAL IS IT
Appeal work is strongly seasonal within each state, but the states peak at different times, so a multi-state firm has a staggered year and a single-state firm has one sharp peak.

Calendar by jurisdiction (filing deadline, then hearings):
- Texas: file by 15 May (or 30 days after notice). Travis sent 93.2% of residential notices by 15 April 2025, so intake peaks mid-April to mid-May. Hearings: HCAD says its peak is "generally from May to August" and certified its roll on 16 August 2024; Dallas ARB starts in early May and typically finishes by mid-July, with limited hearings October to April. This is the sharpest and largest peak found.
- New Jersey: 15 January (Burlington, Gloucester, Monmouth), 1 April (most), 1 May (revaluation towns). Hearing months not confirmed.
- Ohio (Cuyahoga): file 1 January to 31 March. Hearing months not confirmed.
- New York: one day per town, mostly the fourth Tuesday in May (Suffolk third Tuesday in May, Westchester third Tuesday in June, villages February; Nassau by 1 March, NYC by 15 March). SCAR must start within 30 days of the final roll.
- Georgia: 45 days from the mailing of the notice; dates vary by county.
- Washington (King): 1 July or 60 days after the notice; hearings in order of receipt, "6 months or more" later, by phone or on the record.
- California (LA): file 2 July to 30 November; hearing timing not confirmed.
- Florida: TRIM notices usually mid-August, petition within 25 days (about mid-September); hearings can start no earlier than about October (inference from the 25-day notice rule).
- Illinois (Cook, Board of Review): rolling 30-day township windows; in 2026 four groups ran from 3 August to 8 December with only 22 of 38 townships named so far, so the season runs into 2027.

VOLUMES AND CONCENTRATION
No jurisdiction I opened publishes monthly hearing counts. Annual figures: Harris 516,205 protests (2024); Travis about 210,038 protests (2025), 86% agent-filed; Cook Board of Review 290,533 appeals (tax year 2025); King County 5,000 to 7,500 a year (2012-2017). In Travis only about one residential protest in ten went to a formal hearing (19,459 hearings against 189,382 protests in 2025, my ratio); most were settled by agreement. So for capacity planning in Texas the settlement and informal workload is about nine times the formal hearing count, and the formal hearings are compressed into roughly ten to fifteen weeks (early May to mid-August, from the Dallas and Harris statements).

NOTICE LEAD TIMES
Texas: at least 15 days. Florida: at least 25 days (15 for a rescheduled hearing). King County: 22 business days per the state review, with evidence due 21 business days before. Cuyahoga: evidence up to 7 business days before; notice lead time not found. So a firm typically learns its Texas hearing dates only about two weeks ahead, which limits how far consultant schedules can be fixed in advance.

BATCHING FOR ONE AGENT
Texas statute is the only explicit rule found: on request, up to 20 designated properties must be heard consecutively on the same day, and more than one such request may be filed per year; the ARB may also line up all of an agent's protests consecutively in a fixed order. Counterweights: seniors' and disabled owners' hearings are scheduled before agent hearings, and a hearing that has not started within two hours can be postponed on request (Florida has a similar two-hour rule). I found no published batching rule for the other states.

PLANNING IMPLICATIONS (inference, not sourced fact)
- For a Texas-centred firm the new process needs to be live before appraisal notices go out, that is by late March or early April, because intake peaks mid-April to 15 May and hearings begin in early May. Counting back from today (18 November 2026) that leaves about four months before the 2027 season.
- For New Jersey January-calendar counties and Cuyahoga the filing window opens 1 January, so anything serving those markets needs to be ready by end of December.
- Florida and Cook County are autumn peaks and LA filing closes 30 November; these could use off-season Texas capacity.
- Peak capacity should be sized on formal hearings per consultant-day (up to 20 per same-day request in Texas) plus a much larger settlement workload, not on protest counts alone.
