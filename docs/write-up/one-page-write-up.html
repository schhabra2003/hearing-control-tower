Hearing coordination at Alice's Law Firm

Shreyaansh Chhabra · FairAppeal take-home, November 2026

The problem. Hearings are missed because the firm's records drift from the county's official record and consultants are not confirmed in writing in time. In the example week, 4 of the 11 hearings due within three working days had no consultant's written yes, and 11 of 24 records did not match (measured). Missed hearings are not tracked.

Recommendation. First, make every hearing READY three working days ahead: five checks, each closed by its owner's written reply, kept in one shared spreadsheet, with no custom software. Next, recruit consultants, because they, not coordinators, limit growth. Build the control tower (software that runs each hearing) only from about 5,000 hearings a year, once the firm's own timed minutes prove it pays.

2. Priorities and why

First · from December

Every hearing READY three working days out. A missed hearing costs the client and the firm, so reliability comes before volume.

Next · December to April

More consultants, fuller days. Today's 6 leave no spare. Twenty recruiting calls a week target two per office by mid-March; booking an office's whole day lifts a consultant-day from 1.7 toward 2.4.

Later · September

Decide on the control tower by the rule in section 5.

4. Where people decide; proof that work is done

  • People decide; AI only suggests. Coordinator sends offers; Alice approves terms; counsel rules on who may appear and sign. On 100 unseen test messages, agreed AI readings were right 73 of 81 times (90%, measured), so a person checks each.
  • Status is computed, never typed; each check links to the message that proves it.
  • A hearing closes only with its result and any signed agreement on file.
  • Every Friday, a second person opens every proof link of 10 hearings.

6. Assumptions, people, tools and budget

Assumptions. 300 hearings a year (estimate; 240 to 320); consultant $90 a hearing, coordinator $33 an hour (sourced).

People. Associate 2 to 4 weeks, coordinator 3 days, Alice 2 hours a week, counsel about 5 hours, technical help 3 days.

Tools. Shared spreadsheet and mailbox, text line ($1,000 a year), two AI models (under $80 a year).

Budget. Set-up about $12,000 at 300 hearings, $23,000 at 1,000 (payroll included). At 5,000 hearings, about $330,000 of cash is tied up at peak (estimates).

5. Scaling without linear human work

Local processes are data, not code. Each office's rules are one row; a new county takes about 8 hours of set-up (estimate). A load test held 39 offices and 1,199 hearings (measured).

Consultants, not coordinators, limit growth. At 5,000 hearings a year a peak week needs 95 to 157 consultants but only 3.3 coordinators.

Estimates. Peak week: 60% of a year's hearings in 8 weeks.

When to build the tower. A lean build costs $40,000 to $70,000 a year. At coordinator pay it breaks even at 5,000 to 8,700 hearings a year; in cash it still loses $16,000 to $46,000 at 5,000 (estimates). Build it once timed minutes confirm the 11 and volume reaches that range.
